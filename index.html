Call for Papers

Smart homes are a stack, not a product.

Modern smart homes are built from much more than a single automation platform. They combine connected devices, open firmware, communication protocols, message brokers, orchestration, data storage, observability, edge intelligence, energy management and self-hosted infrastructure.

The Smart Homes Devroom brings these layers — and the communities behind them — together. We invite proposals that demonstrate how Free and Open Source Software can create smart homes that are interoperable, local-first, privacy-respecting, resilient and free from vendor lock-in.

Suggested topics

Show us how the pieces fit together.

The list below is deliberately broad. If your work helps build an open, interoperable and user-controlled smart home, it probably belongs here.

01

Platforms & automation

Home automation platforms, architectures, orchestration, workflow tools and integrations across the stack.

02

Devices & embedded

Open hardware, embedded systems, device firmware, sensors, actuators and maintainable connected devices.

03

Interoperability & messaging

Matter, Thread, Zigbee, Z-Wave, MQTT, event-driven infrastructure, bridges, gateways and protocol integration.

04

Infrastructure & observability

Self-hosting, homelabs, data storage, monitoring, observability, visualisation and infrastructure for the smart home.

05

Sensing & edge intelligence

Local voice assistants, edge AI, computer vision, presence and occupancy detection, context-aware automation.

06

Energy & sustainability

Energy monitoring and optimisation, solar integration, batteries, smart EV charging and energy-aware automation.

07

Privacy, security & resilience

Offline operation, security, reliability, maintainability, reverse engineering and liberating cloud-dependent devices.

08

Real-world lessons

Working implementations, surprising failures, trade-offs, migrations, experiments and lessons others can reuse.

09

What comes next?

Emerging technologies, new architectural ideas and meaningful future directions for open smart homes.

What we are looking for

Teach us something we can use.

  • Address a clearly defined technical or practical problem.
  • Demonstrate working integrations between open technologies.
  • Explain architectural decisions and their trade-offs.
  • Share concrete experiences, including challenges and failures.
  • Explore interoperability beyond individual products or platforms.
  • Help users retain ownership of devices, data and automations.
  • Provide knowledge others can reuse in their own projects.
  • Introduce lesser-known projects that complement the wider ecosystem.
  • Present recent developments or a meaningful future direction.
Project introductions are welcome.
Especially for projects that may be unfamiliar to the wider FOSDEM audience. Go beyond a feature overview: explain the problem, your technical approach and what attendees can learn from it.

Talks should be technically meaningful while remaining understandable to people who may not yet know every part of the smart-home stack. Please make the intended audience, assumed knowledge and key takeaways clear in your proposal.

Live and recorded demos are welcome. Make sure the core of your talk still works if the demo decides to become unexpectedly smart on the day.

Who should submit?

Maintainers, hackers, researchers and people who actually run this stuff at home.

We welcome submissions from developers, maintainers, contributors, researchers and experienced enthusiasts. Professional and hobbyist perspectives are equally valuable when the subject is rooted in Free and Open Source Software.

Talk formats

Two formats. Both leave time for questions.

30 min

Standard talk

25 min presentation3 min Q&A2 min changeover

For architectural stories, integrations, technical deep dives, project developments and substantial real-world lessons.

15 min

Short talk

10 min presentation3 min Q&A2 min changeover

For focused topics, compact demos, new projects, one strong idea, experiments, failures and lessons learned.

Choose the format that best fits your proposal. When building the final programme, the organisers may suggest the other format if it helps us create the strongest possible schedule.

How to submit

Submit through the official FOSDEM system.

FOSDEM's 2027 talk submission system is expected to open around 27 October. As soon as the official submission link is available, this page will link directly to it.

Your proposal should make clear:

  • Talk title and concise abstract
  • The problem you are addressing and your technical approach
  • What attendees will learn or be able to reuse
  • Intended audience and assumed knowledge
  • Speaker name and short biography
  • Preferred format: Standard Talk or Short Talk
  • Relevant project, repository or documentation links where applicable

Technical, open and vendor-neutral

Bring substance, not marketing.

The Smart Homes Devroom is strictly non-commercial. Vendor-neutral technical contributions are welcome, but product pitches, sales presentations and marketing-led talks are not.

Proposals will be evaluated on their technical substance, openness, relevance, originality and value to the FOSDEM community.
